Headshot wardrobe guide

What to Wear for a Professional Headshot

The best headshot wardrobe supports your face, your role, and the place the image will be used. It should feel polished and recognizable, not like a costume chosen only for the camera.

  2. Journal
  3. What to Wear for a Professional Headshot

Written by Ja'Von Williams, Houston photographer

Published August 27, 2026 · 5 minute read

Dress for the Audience and the Role

Start with where the headshot will appear and who needs to trust it. A law-firm bio, creative-founder website, speaking profile, real-estate page, and LinkedIn update can call for different levels of formality. Choose clothing you would realistically wear when meeting that audience on an important day.

If one session needs to serve several uses, bring a structured layer such as a blazer or jacket. A simple change can create a more formal version and a more approachable version without rebuilding the entire look.

Prioritize Fit, Neckline, and Clean Lines

Fit matters more than a label. Clothing that pulls, bunches, gaps, or constantly needs adjustment can distract you and limit posing. Make sure the neckline sits cleanly and the shoulders fit well, since the upper part of the outfit is most visible in a typical crop.

Press or steam clothing before arrival. Bring garments on hangers and pack a lint roller. If you are uncertain between two options, bring both and compare them against the chosen background before the session begins.

Use Color and Pattern Deliberately

Solid colors and subtle textures are dependable because they keep attention on the face. Very small stripes, tight checks, oversized logos, or high-contrast patterns can become distracting. That does not mean every headshot needs neutral clothing. A strong brand color can work well when it fits your identity and does not disappear into the background.

  • Coordinate with the planned background instead of matching it exactly
  • Bring one reliable solid option
  • Keep jewelry and accessories intentional
  • Avoid last-minute clothing that has not been tried on

Plan Glasses, Hair, Makeup, and Grooming

Wear glasses if they are part of how people normally see you. Clean the lenses and, if possible, bring a backup pair. For hair, makeup, and facial grooming, aim for a polished version of your usual professional appearance. Schedule major changes early enough that you feel comfortable with them before the session.

Selected photographs receive natural retouching, but good preparation creates a stronger and more believable result than relying on heavy edits.

Bring Options Without Bringing the Whole Closet

Two complete options are usually more useful than a bag of unrelated pieces. Photograph the combinations on your phone before packing them. Include any industry-specific uniform, lab coat, chef jacket, or branded layer only when it is relevant to how the finished portrait will be used.
