How Often Should You Update Team Headshots in Houston

Team headshots go stale faster than most businesses expect. A simple refresh schedule, tied to real changes rather than guesswork, keeps your website and directory looking current year round.
Written by Ja'Von Williams, Houston photographer · Published September 18, 2026 · 5 minute read

Tie the Timeline to Real Changes, Not a Fixed Calendar

As a general rule, plan to refresh team headshots every one to two years. That said, the better trigger is a real change, a rebrand, a new office, a shift in dress code, or simply a team that looks noticeably different than the photos on your website.

Houston companies with steady hiring often find it easier to schedule a standing annual session rather than trying to track individual expiration dates for each employee photo.

Watch for the Signs a Refresh Is Overdue

A few signals make the need obvious: mismatched backgrounds across the team page, a noticeable style gap between older and newer photos, or employees whose current appearance no longer matches their headshot. Any of these undercut the polished, trustworthy impression a directory or About page is meant to create.

If your headshots are being used across LinkedIn, proposals, press materials, and your website, inconsistency shows up in more places than most teams realize.

Decide Between an On-Site Day and Studio Sessions

An on-site session brings the photographer to your Houston office, which works well for larger teams and minimizes time away from work. A studio session offers more controlled lighting and backdrop options for smaller groups or leadership portraits.

The on-site corporate headshots guide walks through space, scheduling, and setup considerations in more detail if your team is deciding between the two formats.

Keep New Hires From Falling Behind

New employees should be photographed within their first few weeks rather than waiting for the next full team refresh. A placeholder photo or a missing headshot on the team page reads as an oversight to clients and prospects browsing your site.

Some Houston businesses build this into onboarding directly, pairing a quick individual session with the employee's first week so nothing gets missed between larger refresh cycles.

Build Consistency Across the Whole Team

Matching background, lighting, framing, and retouching style across every employee is what makes a team page look intentional rather than assembled from mismatched sources. This consistency matters just as much at a trade show or conference activation, where headshots are often captured in a single high-volume session.

Simple, consistent wardrobe guidance also helps. The headshot wardrobe guide gives employees a clear starting point so the whole group photographs consistently, even without a strict dress code.
