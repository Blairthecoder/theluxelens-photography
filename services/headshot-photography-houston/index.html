Headshots for Every Reason You Need One

Headshot sessions cover a wide range of people: attorneys updating a firm bio, agents building trust with a new listing, consultants who need something warmer than a phone selfie, actors and models who need casting-ready proofs, and founders who need one strong portrait for a deck or a press mention.

Most people booking a headshot need more than a single photograph. Expression, posture, and lighting all change how approachable or authoritative someone reads, so planning covers how the image will be used, whether the tone should feel warm or formal, and how many finished looks are worth capturing in the time available.

The direction stays specific rather than vague. Instead of being told to relax, you get concrete adjustments, chin position, shoulder angle, where your hands rest, so the awkwardness of being photographed fades within the first few frames rather than lingering through the whole session.

Finished images are cropped and sized cleanly enough to work across a website bio, a LinkedIn profile, a printed directory, or a press kit without needing a second round of editing before you can use them.

Planning a Headshot That Works

What to Wear

Solid colors tend to photograph better than busy patterns, and a tone that contrasts gently with your skin and the background reads best on screen. If you're unsure between two tops, bring both.

Corporate vs. Personal Headshots

A corporate headshot usually calls for a more formal, consistent setup, especially when it needs to sit next to teammates' photos in a directory. A personal-brand headshot has more room for personality, a slight turn, a genuine expression, a background that says something about who you are.

Studio or On-Location

A studio setting gives full control over light and background, which matters most for teams that need every photo to match. An on-location session, in an office or a space with character, adds context but takes a little more planning around available light.

Preparing a Team for Company Headshots

Group sessions move faster when everyone arrives with the same wardrobe guidance and a shared time block. Sharing the plan in advance, including background color and any logo requirements, keeps a dozen headshots from turning into a dozen different looks.

Where the Images Can Be Used

A well-planned headshot works across a website team page, LinkedIn, printed materials, and speaker or panel submissions without needing a different photo for each one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I book a headshot session?

Reach out as soon as you have a rough timeline. Team sessions in particular benefit from extra lead time to coordinate schedules.

Can you photograph a whole team or department?

Yes. Group sessions are scheduled with a shared time block so everyone gets consistent lighting, background, and framing.

Do you offer studio and on-location headshots?

Yes. A studio setting gives the most consistency for teams, while an on-location session can add context like an office or a favorite space.

What should I wear?

Solid colors that contrast gently with your skin tone and the background photograph best. Bring a backup top if you're deciding between two.

Can I get more than one look or background?

Multiple looks can be planned depending on session length. Mention what you have in mind when you inquire so enough time is reserved.

How do I receive the final images?

You'll receive a private online gallery to review and download your finished headshots.

Can these images be used for LinkedIn, websites, and print?

Yes. Images are sized and cropped cleanly enough to work across a website bio, LinkedIn, a printed directory, or a press kit.

How do I inquire about a large team session?

Send an inquiry with your headcount, timeline, and location, and Ja'Von will help you plan the logistics.
